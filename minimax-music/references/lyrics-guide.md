# Lyrics Writing Guide

> Deep dive into the craft of AI-assisted lyrics: meter, rhyme, storytelling, and genre conventions.

---

## The Fundamentals

### Meter & Syllable Count

Consistent meter is the #1 factor separating professional-sounding AI lyrics from amateur ones. Each line within a section should have a similar syllable count to create a singable rhythm.

**Example — Inconsistent (BAD):**
```
[Verse 1]
I walked through the rain that night (8 syllables)
Thinking about everything we used to have and it made me so sad (16 syllables)
You're gone (2 syllables)
```

**Example — Consistent (GOOD):**
```
[Verse 1]
I walked through the rain that night (8)
Remembering when you held me tight (8)
The streetlights flickered one by one (8)
Another day without the sun (8)
```

### Rhyme Schemes

| Scheme | Pattern | Feel | Best For |
|--------|---------|------|----------|
| AABB | Couplets | Simple, catchy, nursery-rhyme | Pop, country, folk |
| ABAB | Alternating | Natural, flowing | Rock, indie, singer-songwriter |
| ABBA | Envelope | Sophisticated, reflective | Ballads, art pop |
| AAAA | Monorhyme | Intense, driving | Hip-hop, rap |
| ABCB | Ballad | Storytelling, loose | Folk, narrative songs |
| ABAC | Near-rhyme | Subtle, sophisticated | Alternative, jazz |

### Line Length Consistency

```
SHORT LINES (4-6 syllables): Urgent, punchy, punk, hip-hop verses
MEDIUM LINES (7-9 syllables): Natural, conversational, pop, rock
LONG LINES (10-14 syllables): Flowing, storytelling, folk, prog
```

## Song Structure Templates

### Standard Pop Structure
```
[Intro]          — 4-8 bars, instrumental or hook tease
[Verse 1]        — 8-16 bars, set the scene
[Pre-Chorus]     — 4-8 bars, build tension
[Chorus]         — 8-16 bars, the hook, the payoff
[Verse 2]        — 8-16 bars, develop the story
[Pre-Chorus]     — 4-8 bars
[Chorus]         — 8-16 bars
[Bridge]         — 8 bars, contrast, new perspective
[Chorus]         — 8-16 bars, maybe bigger/more intense
[Outro]          — 4-8 bars, fade or final statement
```

### Hip-Hop Structure
```
[Intro]           — 4-8 bars, beat intro, ad-lib
[Verse 1]         — 16 bars
[Hook/Chorus]     — 8 bars
[Verse 2]         — 16 bars
[Hook/Chorus]     — 8 bars
[Verse 3]         — 16 bars (optional feature verse)
[Hook/Chorus]     — 8 bars
[Outro]           — 4-8 bars, fade
```

### EDM / Dance Structure
```
[Intro]           — 16-32 bars, build elements
[Build-up]        — 8-16 bars, rising tension
[Drop]            — 16-32 bars, full energy
[Breakdown]       — 8-16 bars, strip back
[Build-up]        — 8-16 bars
[Drop]            — 16-32 bars, variation on first drop
[Outro]           — 16-32 bars, DJ-friendly outro
```

### Ballad Structure
```
[Intro]           — 4-8 bars, piano/guitar
[Verse 1]         — 8-16 bars, intimate, personal
[Chorus]          — 8-16 bars, emotional peak
[Verse 2]         — 8-16 bars, deeper story
[Chorus]          — 8-16 bars
[Bridge]          — 8 bars, emotional climax
[Chorus]          — 8-16 bars, soft then build
[Outro]           — 4-8 bars, resolution
```

## Storytelling Architecture

### The Three-Act Song Structure

**Act 1 — Setup (Verse 1)**
- Establish who, what, where
- Introduce the core conflict or question
- End with a line that makes the chorus feel earned

**Act 2 — Development (Verse 2 + Bridge)**
- Deepen the story — add complication or new perspective
- The bridge should surprise: a twist, a realization, a change of heart
- Add concrete details (specifics > abstractions)

**Act 3 — Resolution (Final Chorus + Outro)**
- The emotional conclusion
- The final chorus often intensifies: bigger arrangement, added harmonies, lyrical variation
- The outro provides closure: a final line, a repeated hook, a fade

### The Power of Specifics

| Weak (Abstract) | Strong (Specific) |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| "I remember the good times" | "I remember Tuesday mornings, coffee on the fire escape" |
| "You were so beautiful" | "You had paint stains on your jeans and sunlight in your hair" |
| "I felt so alone" | "Third floor walkup, single lightbulb, nobody calling my name" |
| "The city was busy" | "Taxi horns on Broadway, steam rising from the grating" |

## Genre-Specific Lyric Conventions

### Pop
- **Theme:** Love, empowerment, celebration, heartbreak
- **Language:** Simple, universal, relatable
- **Hook:** Repetitive, catchy, easy to remember (1-2 phrases)
- **Rhyme:** AABB or ABAB, tight and predictable
- **Avoid:** Overly complex metaphors, obscure references

### Indie/Alternative
- **Theme:** Introspection, alienation, observational, ironic
- **Language:** Conversational, literary, sometimes abstract
- **Hook:** Can be subtle, doesn't need to be obvious
- **Rhyme:** May use near-rhyme or abandon rhyme entirely
- **Embrace:** Ambiguity, understatement, specific imagery

### Hip-Hop
- **Theme:** Personal narrative, social commentary, bravado, wordplay
- **Language:** Dense, rhythmic, clever wordplay, cultural references
- **Flow:** Syllable count and stress patterns are critical
- **Rhyme:** Complex — internal rhyme, multisyllabic, slant rhyme
- **Structure:** 16-bar verses are standard

### Country
- **Theme:** Storytelling, small-town life, love, loss, trucks, beer 😄
- **Language:** Conversational, plain-spoken, colorful idioms
- **Hook:** Clear, memorable, often the song title
- **Rhyme:** AABB or ABCB, usually tight
- **Details:** Concrete details (brand names, places, objects) are essential

### R&B / Soul
- **Theme:** Love, sensuality, relationships, emotional vulnerability
- **Language:** Smooth, melodic, sometimes poetic
- **Hook:** Melodic, emotional, often melismatic
- **Rhyme:** Can be loose — the melody carries the weight
- **Style:** Ad-libs, runs, vocal embellishments implied

### Rock
- **Theme:** Rebellion, freedom, angst, love, existential
- **Language:** Direct, sometimes aggressive, anthemic
- **Hook:** Often shouted or belted, guitar-driven
- **Rhyme:** AABB or ABAB, but raw is fine
- **Energy:** The delivery matters as much as the words

### Electronic / Dance
- **Theme:** Unity, escape, euphoria, the moment
- **Language:** Simple, repetitive, sometimes vocoded
- **Hook:** A single phrase repeated, processed
- **Rhyme:** Less important — repetition and rhythm are key
- **Structure:** Built around drops, not verses

## Lyrics Quality Checklist

Before finalizing lyrics:

- [ ] **Consistent meter:** Each line in a section has similar syllable count (±2)
- [ ] **Natural rhyme:** Rhymes feel organic, not forced
- [ ] **Emotional arc:** Story progresses from setup through development to resolution
- [ ] **Memorable hook:** The chorus contains a phrase you'd want to sing along to
- [ ] **Specific imagery:** At least 2-3 concrete, sensory details
- [ ] **Structure tags:** Appropriate tags used correctly (`[Verse]`, `[Chorus]`, etc.)
- [ ] **Line breaks:** Each line is a natural breath/musical phrase
- [ ] **Genre fit:** Language and themes match the intended genre
- [ ] **No clichés:** Fresh imagery > tired phrases (unless genre demands it)
- [ ] **Singability:** Read it aloud — does it flow naturally?

## When to Use AI Lyrics vs. Human-Written

| Use AI Lyrics When | Use Human Lyrics When |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| User has no lyrics and wants full song | User has specific lyrics they've written |
| User has a theme but needs creative jumpstart | User wants exact words/phrases preserved |
| User wants multiple variations to choose from | Lyrics carry personal significance |
| Rapid prototyping / demo creation | Final release-quality track |
| Background music where lyrics are secondary | Artist-branded content |
